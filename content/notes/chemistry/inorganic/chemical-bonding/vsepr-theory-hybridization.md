## 1. Steric Number & Hybridization Rule

The steric number ($\text{SN}$) determines the electron domain geometry and hybridization state around the central atom:

$$\text{SN} = (\text{Number of bonded atoms}) + (\text{Number of lone pairs on central atom})$$

| Steric Number ($\text{SN}$) | Hybridization | Ideal Bond Angle | Electron Domain Geometry | Example Molecule |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **2** | $sp$ | $180^\circ$ | Linear | $\ce{BeCl2}, \ce{CO2}$ |
| **3** | $sp^2$ | $120^\circ$ | Trigonal Planar | $\ce{BF3}, \ce{SO3}$ |
| **4** | $sp^3$ | $109.5^\circ$ | Tetrahedral | $\ce{CH4}, \ce{NH4+}$ |
| **5** | $sp^3d$ | $90^\circ, 120^\circ$ | Trigonal Bipyramidal | $\ce{PCl5}, \ce{SF4}$ |
| **6** | $sp^3d^2$ | $90^\circ$ | Octahedral | $\ce{SF6}, \ce{XeF4}$ |

## 2. VSEPR Lone Pair Repulsion Hierarchy

Electron pairs repel each other according to the following magnitude order:

$$\text{Lone Pair -- Lone Pair} > \text{Lone Pair -- Bond Pair} > \text{Bond Pair -- Bond Pair}$$

Because unshared lone pairs are held closer to the central nucleus, they exert stronger angular repulsion, compressing adjacent bond angles:
* **Methane ($\ce{CH4}$)**: 0 lone pairs $\implies 109.5^\circ$ (Tetrahedral)
* **Ammonia ($\ce{NH3}$)**: 1 lone pair $\implies 107.0^\circ$ (Trigonal Pyramidal)
* **Water ($\ce{H2O}$)**: 2 lone pairs $\implies 104.5^\circ$ (Bent)

### Lewis Acid-Base Adduct Formation
Coordination of a lone pair into an empty orbital alters central atom hybridization:

$$\ce{NH3(g) + BF3(g) -> H3N-BF3(s)}$$

## 3. High-Yield Exam Tricks
1. **Double and Triple Bonds**: Count as a **single electron domain** when calculating the steric number ($\text{SN}$).
2. **Equatorial vs. Axial in Trigonal Bipyramidal ($sp^3d$)**: Lone pairs ALWAYS occupy **equatorial positions** to minimize $90^\circ$ repulsions (e.g., $\ce{SF4}$ has a See-Saw shape, $\ce{ClF3}$ has a T-shape, $\ce{XeF2}$ is Linear).

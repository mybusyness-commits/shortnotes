Topic 1: INTRODUCTION TO CHEMICAL EQUILIBRIUM

In a reversible chemical reaction, the $\text{rate of forward reaction} =$ to the $\text{rate of backward reaction}$, it is said to be in chemical equilibrium.

Characteristics of Chemical Equilibrium:

  • The reaction never stops; it's just that the forward and backward processes happen at identical speeds.
  • The concentration of reactants and products remains constant over time.
  • It can only be achieved in a closed system. (In a closed system, no exchange of matter takes place. Energy exchange can happen).

Chemical equilibrium tells you how fast a reaction reaches equilibrium, while thermodynamics tells you where the equilibrium lies. So they are both connected, and hence it is necessary to know a few basic concepts of thermodynamics.

Ideal Gas Law:
$$PV = nRT$$

Ideal gas vs real gas:

  • In ideal gases: mass is negligible; real gas molecules have some mass.
  • Ideal gas molecules: no interaction with each other, real gas molecules have some interaction.

Internal energy is the total sum of all microscopic kinetic and potential energies within the thermodynamic system.

Enthalpy tells us whether a reaction absorbs heat (endothermic) or releases heat (exothermic).

Entropy measures the degree of disorder or randomness. It is like how randomly the molecules are moving. So solids will have low entropy because their particles are fixed. While gases have high entropy.
* Entropy increases with increase in volume. More volume means more space, so more randomness.
* Entropy increases with increase in temperature. More temperature gives more kinetic energy causing molecules to move faster causing more randomness.

Gibbs free energy ($G$)

  • In nature, we prefer the lowest possible energy state. So a reaction proceeds in the direction that lowers its free energy until $G = 0$.
  • At chemical equilibrium, $G = 0$.
  • If $G < 0$, then the reaction is spontaneous and releases energy to proceed forward.
  • If $G > 0$, then the reaction is non-spontaneous and requires external energy to occur.

Now, we introduce a term called ‘CHEMICAL POTENTIAL’ ($\mu$ / neu)

It is defined as the change in the total Gibbs free energy ($G$) of a system when a tiny amount of specific substance ($n$) is added while keeping temperature and pressure constant.

$$\text{Nue } (\mu) = \frac{\Delta G}{n}$$

The higher the chemical potential of a molecule, the more unstable or eager the molecule is to react. Let’s understand with an example.

Consider two tanks, A and B. A is completely filled with water, which means the pressure at the bottom is very high. B only has a few inches of water at the bottom. Now, Tank A is said to have high chemical potential – the water is under a lot of pressure and has a strong escaping tendency. If we now open a valve between the two tanks, water will naturally flow from Tank A to Tank B.

So, reactants with high chemical potential want to turn into products to relieve that pressure.

Now, as water pours into Tank B, its level rises (chemical potential increases), while Tank A’s level drops (chemical potential goes down). Eventually both reach equilibrium.
